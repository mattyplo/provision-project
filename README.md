# The Provision Project

A public, source-backed explorer of provisions in the U.S. federal tax code.

## The question

For each provision, The Provision Project asks:

- What does it do?
- Who qualifies for it?
- When and how did it enter the tax code?
- Why did Congress create it?
- How has it changed over time?
- Who economically benefits from it?
- What is its estimated fiscal effect?

The goal is not to label provisions as good, bad, wasteful, corrupt, or loopholes. The goal is to make the history, mechanics, beneficiaries, and evidence legible enough for readers to make their own judgments.

## MVP

The first milestone is five complete provision records covering a diverse set of tax-code mechanisms. Those records will help refine the research methodology, source model, data schema, and eventual public interface before attempting broader coverage.

## Research principles

The project prioritizes primary and nonpartisan official sources: statutory text and Public Laws, congressional committee reports, the Joint Committee on Taxation (JCT), Treasury and IRS, the Congressional Budget Office (CBO), and the Government Accountability Office (GAO).

Every research record should clearly distinguish statutory facts, government estimates, economic analysis, arguments made by supporters or critics, and our own technical inferences.
