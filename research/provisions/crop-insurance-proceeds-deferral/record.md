# Crop-insurance proceeds deferral

**Provision ID:** crop-insurance-proceeds-deferral  
**Current citation:** 26 U.S.C. § 451(f)  
**Current-law snapshot:** October 27, 2026  
**Research status:** Initial complete MVP record; source audit completed with documented limitations  
**Primary audit:** [Source audit](source-audit.md) · [Research comparison](comparison.md)

## At a glance

A qualifying cash-method taxpayer may elect to include certain crop-insurance proceeds and specified disaster payments in income in the **tax year following the year the crop was damaged or destroyed**, rather than the year the payment was received. The election generally requires that, under the taxpayer's customary business practice, income from the affected crop would have been reported in a following tax year. This is a **timing rule**, not a permanent tax exemption. [26 U.S.C. § 451(f)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t26/s451/); [26 C.F.R. § 1.451-6](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.451-6).

**Why it exists (documented rationale):** The Senate Finance Committee explained in 1969 that immediate recognition of crop-insurance proceeds could place both an insurance payment and the next crop's receipts in the same tax year, even where the farmer normally would have sold the destroyed crop in the following year. The committee described the election as addressing that mismatch and avoiding the complications of subsequent loss carrybacks. [S. Rep. No. 91-552 (1969), pp. 106–107](source-audit.md#direct-verification-senate-finance-committee-report-no-91-552-1969).

**Fiscal effect:** No independently attributable official dollar estimate for § 451(f) was identified in the public sources examined. This **does not** imply zero fiscal effect. [Source audit, A-21–A-23](source-audit.md#additional-fiscal-source-checks--treasury-fy2027-and-2014-repeal-proposal-october-27-2026).

**Economic incidence:** Unknown / insufficient direct evidence. The eligible taxpayer is not necessarily the ultimate economic beneficiary.

## Scope and current-law status

This record concerns the election for **crop-insurance proceeds and certain statutorily specified disaster payments** under § 451(f). It does not attempt to describe the federal crop-insurance subsidy program as a whole, general agricultural disaster relief, farm-income averaging, or the separate weather-related livestock-sale election now in § 451(g). The crop-insurance election was enacted in 1969 as § 451(d) and redesignated § 451(f) in 2017. [26 U.S.C. § 451](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t26/s451/); [Pub. L. 91-172, § 215](https://uscode.house.gov/statutes/pl/91/172.pdf); [Pub. L. 115-97, § 13221](https://uscode.house.gov/statutes/pl/115/97.pdf).

**Operative regime as of October 27, 2026:** § 451(f) remains the current statutory election, subject to its conditions and the implementing regulation. The audited sources identify no enacted future-effective repeal or sunset for this election. The 2014 Tax Reform Act proposal discussed below was **not enacted**. This is a dated description, not a guarantee that future legislation will leave the rule unchanged.

## What the election does

Ordinarily, cash-method taxpayers recognize income when received. Section 451(f) permits a qualifying taxpayer who receives crop-insurance proceeds in the year of crop damage or destruction to **elect** to include qualifying proceeds in income in the following tax year, if income from the crop would ordinarily have been reported in that following year. [26 U.S.C. § 451(f)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t26/s451/).

For example, consider a farmer who customarily harvests a crop in the fall and sells it the next spring. If the crop is destroyed and the farmer receives qualifying insurance proceeds in the fall, the election can align recognition of those proceeds with the year in which the crop income would normally have been reported. **This is an illustrative application, not a finding about a particular taxpayer.**

The regulation makes clear that proceeds **received in the year after the crop damage** are already recognized in that later year under the ordinary cash-method rule; the election does **not** allow another year's postponement. [26 C.F.R. § 1.451-6(a)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.451-6).

### Who qualifies?

- **Accounting method:** The taxpayer must use the cash method for the relevant trade or business. [26 U.S.C. § 451(f)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t26/s451/).
- **Nature of payment:** The proceeds must arise from qualifying crop destruction or damage, or fall within the specified statutory disaster-payment treatment. Not every agricultural payment qualifies. [26 U.S.C. § 451(f)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t26/s451/).
- **Customary sales practice:** Under the taxpayer's established practice, the crop's income would normally have been reported in a later tax year. IRS Publication 225 and the Eighth Circuit's discussion in *Nelson* describe the IRS's **more-than-50-percent** standard for the relevant crop; that numerical test is **an administrative/judicial interpretation**, not text appearing in the statute itself. [IRS Publication 225](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p225); [*Nelson v. Commissioner*, 568 F.3d 662 (8th Cir. 2009), pp. 4–7](https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/09/06/082912P.pdf).
- **Election scope:** The regulation requires consistent treatment of **all qualifying proceeds for crops within a single trade or business**; separate businesses are treated separately. [26 C.F.R. § 1.451-6(a)(2)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.451-6).

### What does not automatically qualify?

The IRS distinguishes insurance attributable to **physical crop damage or yield loss** from proceeds arising **solely from market-price declines**. For a policy combining both types of loss, the IRS's 2009 information letter discusses allocation rather than treating the entire payment as automatically deferrable. The letter is an **IRS information letter, not a revenue ruling or binding judicial holding**. Current Publication 225 also excludes certain rainfall-index payments unconnected to physical damage. [IRS Information Letter INFO 2009-0024 (Feb. 26, 2009)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-wd/09-0024.pdf); [IRS Publication 225](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p225).

In *Nelson*, the Eighth Circuit rejected using sales of other crops across the whole farm to establish the customary-sales test for the affected crop. This is an important qualification to simplified descriptions of a whole-farm threshold. [*Nelson*, pp. 6–7](https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/09/06/082912P.pdf).

### How is the election made?

The regulation provides for a statement attached to the taxpayer's return (or amended return) for the year of crop damage or destruction. The statement identifies the crop, customary reporting practice, circumstances of the loss, and insurance payments, among other specified information. Readers should consult the regulation and current filing instructions rather than relying on this summary as filing advice. [26 C.F.R. § 1.451-6(b)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.451-6); [IRS Publication 225](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p225).

## Origin and documented rationale

**Enactment:** Congress enacted the crop-insurance proceeds election in the **Tax Reform Act of 1969**, Pub. L. 91-172, § 215, on **December 30, 1969**, originally codified as § 451(d). The enacted effective-date provision applied to taxable years ending after enactment. [Pub. L. 91-172, § 215](https://uscode.house.gov/statutes/pl/91/172.pdf).

**Contemporaneous committee explanation:** The Senate Finance Committee described a situation in which insurance received when a crop was lost could be recognized in a different year from the year in which the farmer normally would have recognized the crop's sale proceeds. It described the election as permitting a timing match and reducing the need to rely on later loss carrybacks. This is the **committee's stated rationale**, not an independently established account of every legislator's motive. [S. Rep. No. 91-552 (1969), pp. 106–107; direct-inspection notes](source-audit.md#direct-verification-senate-finance-committee-report-no-91-552-1969).

The committee's cited **$25 million annual revenue figure** concerned a **broader farm-loss package**, **not an estimate for this election individually**. [Source audit](source-audit.md#direct-verification-senate-finance-committee-report-no-91-552-1969).

## Fiscal effect and estimate limitations

**Official § 451(f)-specific fiscal estimate: Not identified in examined public sources.**

The examined [Treasury FY2027 tax-expenditures report](https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/tax-policy/tax-expenditures) does not separately identify this election among its agriculture entries. This is a **report-specific negative finding**, not proof of no fiscal effect or of no historical estimate. For timing provisions, an annual cash-flow estimate can differ materially from a present-value estimate.

The **2014 JCT estimate of +$10.4 billion over FY2014–2023** concerns a **package of proposed income-recognition changes**. It is neither a provision-specific repeal score nor an annual tax-expenditure estimate for § 451(f). It must **not** be attributed to this election. [JCT JCX-20-14](https://www.jct.gov/getattachment/9b4f3ef8-e9b6-4f8f-8132-adb62cf51bf6/x-20-14-4562.pdf); [source audit](source-audit.md#a-23--follow-up-search-for-a-disaggregated-2014-jct-estimate-october-27-2026).

Likewise, IRS-reported insurance receipts and previously deferred income are **not estimates of federal revenue forgone**.

## Cross-program dependencies

Qualifying insurance payments arise in part from crop-insurance arrangements administered in the federal agricultural policy environment, and § 451(f) incorporates certain disaster-assistance categories defined by other federal statutes. The tax election itself is administered through federal income-tax reporting by the IRS. The **payment's underlying cause and legal category** matter to eligibility, but this record does not assess crop-insurance subsidies or USDA program effectiveness. [26 U.S.C. § 451(f)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t26/s451/); [IRS Publication 225](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p225). A more detailed map of USDA/RMA and disaster-program authorities remains an optional expansion rather than an assertion that all such programs qualify.

## Important interpretations and evidence limits

- **Judicial interpretation:** [*Nelson v. Commissioner*, 568 F.3d 662 (8th Cir. 2009)](https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/09/06/082912P.pdf) addresses the customary-sales test and rejects the asserted whole-farm aggregation approach.
- **Administrative interpretation:** [IRS Information Letter INFO 2009-0024](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-wd/09-0024.pdf) distinguishes physical-loss payments from price-only proceeds and discusses allocation in mixed cases. It is not a precedential revenue ruling.
- **Original authorities not directly inspected:** Revenue Ruling 74-145 (1974-1 C.B. 113) and Notice 89-55 (1989-20 I.R.B. 134). Their absence is documented rather than concealed; the former is discussed in *Nelson*. Do not attribute claims directly to an original document that was not obtained.
- **Research correction:** Earlier secondary research misstated several 2023 Schedule F figures and characterized *Nelson* as supporting whole-farm aggregation. The independent [source audit](source-audit.md) corrects those claims.
