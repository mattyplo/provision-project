## Additional independent audit — participation and infant health (2026-11-16)

**C008 — verified source number, corrected interpretation.** [IRS national/state participation table, TY2022, National row and footnotes 1–2](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/eitc-central/eitc-participation-rate-by-state) reports **80.8%** for the **ACS-linked** estimate (model-based eligibility denominator). **The same IRS footnote reports a distinct CPS-based national estimate of 78%**. Therefore the original C008 'national 80.8%' is **ambiguous/misleading unless the ACS methodology is specified**. National headline comparisons should identify which series they use. [CRS R43805 (Sept. 2026)](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-09-14_R43805_98db5efec04e335c27966dc3c98d56cdc45fc1ee.html) rounds the national figure to 81% elsewhere; the IRS table's methodology footnote is controlling for this distinction. **Disposition: Qualified, not unqualified Verified.**

**C013 — original research verified, causal scope qualified.** [Hoynes, Miller and Simon, NBER W18206 (2012), abstract](https://www.nber.org/papers/w18206) and [full working paper](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w18206/w18206.pdf) study tax-reform-induced EITC variation, using difference-in-differences and event-study specifications. Authors report that a policy-induced **$1,000 EITC increase** for **single mothers with ≤12 years of education** was associated with **6.7–10.8% relative reduction** in low birth weight. Authors propose prenatal care and reduced smoking as possible mechanisms; they find little evidence for health-insurance changes. **Disposition: Verified as the study's reported estimate, not an established universal causal effect.** The paper has a published comment and reply; methodological debate requires further review before 'causally established' without qualification.


## Critical C013 version correction (2026-11-16)
The prior C013 entry and follow-up cited **2012 NBER W18206's 6.7–10.8%** estimate. **Superseded for publication by final journal version:** [Hoynes/Miller/Simon, *AEJ: Economic Policy* 2015, pp. 172–211, abstract and pp. 174–175](https://gspp.berkeley.edu/archived/files/research/pdf/Hoynes-Miller-Simon-AEJPolicy-Feb-2015.pdf) reports **0.17–0.31 percentage points**, or **1.6–2.9% relative** (rounded **2–3%**), per **$1,000 policy-induced after-tax income** among single low-education mothers. [Dench/Joyce 2020](https://www.danieldench.com/static/pdfs/published/HE2.pdf) dispute identification; [authors' reply](https://gspp.berkeley.edu/assets/uploads/research/pdf/DJ_response_JHE_012519.pdf) disputes the critique. **C013 status: corrected, qualified, contested causal interpretation.** See [empirical-reconciliation.md](empirical-reconciliation.md). Earlier working-paper figures in this ledger remain visible for provenance, **not as the current recommended result**.
