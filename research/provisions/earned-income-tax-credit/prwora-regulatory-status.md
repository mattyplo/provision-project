# PRWORA refundable-credit proposed rule — regulatory-status resolution

**Research performed:** 2026-11-16. **Legal snapshot:** 2026-10-31. **Docket:** REG-119882-25; RIN 1545-BS06; Federal Register document 2026-16985.

## Conclusion (bounded, source-backed)
**As of October 31, 2026, this regulatory proposal had not been identified as finalized.** The original [August 20, 2026 notice (91 FR 53812)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-20/pdf/FR-2026-08-20.pdf) expressly calls itself a *notice of proposed rulemaking*. The [October 9 hearing cancellation (91 FR 64631)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-09/html/2026-20790.htm) cancels the hearing **only**; it neither finalizes nor withdraws the proposal. [CRS, October 26, 2026, IF13337](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_IF13337_099272ebae8f1285d1e07ab6aa1ffaf844d190d5.html) states explicitly that **IRS had not announced when it intended to issue final regulations**. A search for the exact docket, RIN and rule title did not locate a final rule dated by October 31. This supports **“proposed; no final rule located as of cutoff”** with **high confidence**, not an absolute certification of the entire Federal Register index.

## Why the distinction matters
The proposal would classify the **refunded portion** of EITC and three other refundable tax credits as a *federal public benefit* for PRWORA purposes. It would impose qualified-alien/citizenship status restrictions on that portion, **not** simply repeal the credit or rewrite the underlying §32 earned-income calculation. These are **proposed effects**, not current enacted eligibility conditions established by this notice.

The [proposed regulatory text, 91 FR 53812, p. 53825, proposed §1.32-4(g)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-20/pdf/FR-2026-08-20.pdf) specifies applicability for **taxable years ending on or after the date of publication of final regulations**. Because no final publication was located by Oct. 31, **this proposal itself supplies no Oct. 31 operative date**. If a final rule later appears, its final text and applicability date must be independently checked; the proposal's text does not guarantee what the final rule will say.

## IRS Schedule 3-A is not proof of finalization
[IRS EITC eligibility guidance (accessed Nov. 16)](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/individuals/earned-income-tax-credit/who-qualifies-for-the-earned-income-tax-credit-eitc) says taxpayers claiming TY2026 EITC **“may have to complete Schedule 3-A, Federal Public Benefit.”** [CRS Oct. 26](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_IF13337_099272ebae8f1285d1e07ab6aa1ffaf844d190d5.html) describes **draft Schedule 3-A** and a draft Form 1040 immigration-status question, *while also describing the regulation as proposed*. Forms can be developed in anticipation of rules. **Neither draft forms nor anticipatory IRS web guidance are a final Treasury decision published in the Federal Register.** The IRS page is live and its Nov. 16 content should not be backdated to Oct. 31.

## Recommended provision-record wording
> **Regulatory development (proposed as of October 31, 2026):** In August 2026 Treasury and the IRS proposed regulations that would treat the refunded portion of the EITC as a federal public benefit subject to certain immigration-status restrictions under PRWORA. A scheduled hearing was cancelled in October, and CRS reported on October 26 that the IRS had not announced a final-rule timetable. No final rule was identified by the October 31 research cutoff. The proposal's own applicability clause depends on publication of final regulations. IRS draft forms and guidance mentioning Schedule 3-A do not by themselves establish that the proposal took effect.

**Disposition:** C014 **resolved for publication as a properly qualified proposed-rule statement**. Do not describe the proposed restriction as operative law or imply a 2026 tax-year effective date. **Optional ongoing verification:** periodically check the Federal Register for RIN 1545-BS06 and any final Treasury decision; if published, revise the record and date the change.
