# Empirical research reconciliation — publication version, critiques, and incidence

**Audit:** 2026-11-16. This document distinguishes published papers from earlier working-paper vintages and distinguishes claims from evidence. It is a source-reconciliation supplement to [empirical-evidence-audit.md](empirical-evidence-audit.md).

## Material correction: infant health estimate changed across versions

The archived claim C013 and earlier audit quote **6.7–10.8% relative low-birth-weight reduction per $1,000**, from [Hoynes, Miller and Simon, NBER W18206 (2012) abstract](https://www.nber.org/papers/w18206). **That is not the headline estimate in the final published paper.**

The authors' [published *American Economic Journal: Economic Policy* 7(1), 2015, pp. 172–211](https://gspp.berkeley.edu/archived/files/research/pdf/Hoynes-Miller-Simon-AEJPolicy-Feb-2015.pdf), abstract and printed pp. 174–175, report for **single mothers with ≤12 years' education** a **0.17–0.31 percentage-point** decline in low birth weight, from a baseline around **10.7%**: **1.6–2.9% relative decline**, per **$1,000 policy-induced increase in after-tax income**. The published article's abstract rounds to **2–3%**. This is materially smaller than the 2012 working-paper abstract's **6.7–10.8%**, and the exposure denominator is described as **after-tax income** rather than merely credit received.

**Editorial disposition:** C013 **corrected and qualified**. Cite the **2015 published paper** for the main quantitative claim; mention the earlier working-paper estimate only when explaining version history. Never combine the two estimates or imply 6.7–10.8% is the published result.

## Directly inspected methodological dispute

**Original design:** [Hoynes/Miller/Simon (2015), printed pp. 173–175](https://gspp.berkeley.edu/archived/files/research/pdf/Hoynes-Miller-Simon-AEJPolicy-Feb-2015.pdf) use 1984–1998 US natality records, differences across birth order / family size, a 1993 reform difference-in-differences, event studies, and a multi-reform fixed-effects design. Their authors acknowledge that induced earnings and tax-transfer income cannot be fully disentangled (p. 173 n.3). They estimate treatment-on-treated income using CPS/TAXSIM, not actual EITC amounts linked to each birth.

**Critique:** [Dench and Joyce, *Health Economics* 29(1) (2020), pp. 72–84](https://www.danieldench.com/static/pdfs/published/HE2.pdf) argue that 1990 expansions contaminate some 1993 pre-period comparisons; certain placebo and parallel-trends checks fail; estimated smoking mechanisms are implausible; and changing national policies or the waning crack epidemic may confound effects. These are **authors' criticisms**, not established refutations.

**Authors' reply:** [Hoynes/Miller/Simon, *Report on “The Earned Income Tax Credit and Infant Health Revisited”*, pp. 1–4 and response tables](https://gspp.berkeley.edu/assets/uploads/research/pdf/DJ_response_JHE_012519.pdf) defend selecting 1991–93 pre-period because much of the 1990 expansion occurred earlier; say the critique substitutes a parity-2 vs parity-1 contrast for their main parity-2+ vs parity-1 contrast; and defend additional parity-3+ vs parity-2 comparisons. This is an **author reply**, not independent validation.

**Assessment:** The effect's direction/magnitude and causal identification are **contested**. The project may report a specific published-study estimate and the methodological challenge, but **must not assert infant-health effects are conclusively established**. Distinguish 'reported causal estimate under authors' design' from independent causal certification.

## Employment and wage incidence — cross-study comparability

| Source | Estimand / design | Claim supported | Remaining uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Eissa & Liebman, QJE 1996 / NBER W5158](https://www.nber.org/papers/w5158) | 1986 tax reform, single mothers relative to childless single women, DiD | Relative participation increase ~1.4 pp | TRA86's other provisions and parallel trends; not general all-reform effect |
| [Kleven, *Journal of Public Economics* 2024, article 105135](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0047272724000719) | Multi-reform event-study/specification uncertainty, ~500 estimates | No clear robust employment response in most reform episodes; 1993 is exceptional and potentially confounded | Does not prove structural zero or settle all estimands |
| [Rothstein, *AEJ: Economic Policy* 2010, pp. 177–208](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/pol.2.1.177) | Labor-supply/demand **simulation** | Preferred parameters yield $0.73 after-tax worker income per $1 spending | **Not an observed 27% national employer-capture rate**; worker losses include ineligible workers, model-dependent |
| [Leigh, IZA DP 4960 (2010)](https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/4960/who-benefits-from-the-earned-income-tax-credit-incidence-among-recipients-coworkers-and-firms) | State-policy wage regressions | Wage associations by education group under the study's design | Identification, local labor markets, period, composition and external validity |

**Incidence conclusion:** EITC is legally claimed by eligible taxpayers. There is credible theoretical and empirical motivation to examine wage pass-through, but **no audited nationwide allocation of the credit's economic gains to claimants, employers, or ineligible coworkers**. Publish “economic incidence: unknown / insufficient evidence for a national allocation,” with cited study-specific qualifications.
