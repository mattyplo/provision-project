# EITC final research-quality pass — evidence retention and publication decisions

**Audit date:** 2026-11-16. **Scope:** reconcile available original study records, flag material version changes, preserve divergent findings, and separate publishable descriptive claims from unreviewed causal interpretations. No microdata replication or exhaustive table-by-table robustness verification was performed.

## Editorial rule: retain inconclusive and conflicting results
**Do not suppress an empirical result merely because other research disagrees.** Store each study separately with (a) precise bibliographic/version information, (b) population and period, (c) outcome and estimand, (d) research design, (e) authors' reported finding, (f) methodological caveats/criticisms, (g) source access and inspection level, and (h) status. A project-level synthesis may say 'contested' or 'unknown' **without erasing study-specific findings**. Do not turn non-replication or lack of statistical robustness into proof of a zero effect.

## Critical new version finding: Eissa–Liebman employment estimate
The [1995 NBER W5158 abstract](https://www.nber.org/papers/w5158) reports a **1.4 percentage-point** relative labor-force participation increase comparing 1984–86 with **1988–89**. The [1996 *Quarterly Journal of Economics* published abstract](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/111/2/605/1938452), 111(2):605–637, reports **up to 2.8 percentage points** comparing 1984–86 with **1988–90**. The earlier research ledger's 1.4 pp is **not** the published paper's headline finding. Both are retained as version-specific results; do not conflate the study windows or substitute one for the other. The published full text was not independently reviewed table-by-table. The authors' treatment/control comparison includes other provisions of TRA1986, so the difference cannot automatically be attributed to EITC alone.

## Contrasting published employment results (retain both)
- **Eissa & Liebman (1996), *QJE***: reported up to **+2.8 pp** relative participation, with no relative hours change for those already working. Design: difference-in-differences for unmarried women with children vs unmarried women without children, around TRA1986. [Publisher abstract](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/111/2/605/1938452). **Publisher abstract inspected; complete published methods/tables not independently checked.**
- **Schanzenbach & Strain (2021), *Tax Policy and the Economy* 35:87–129**: report **robust evidence** that federal EITC expansions increased labor-force participation over a long historical horizon. [Published article full-text PDF](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/epdf/10.1086/713494). **Article abstract/executive summary inspected; robustness tables not independently replicated.**
- **Kleven (2024), *Journal of Public Economics* 236:105135**: reports that across ~**500 event-study specifications**, estimated treatment effects are symmetrically distributed around zero, with the **1993** episode exceptional and potentially confounded by welfare reform/macroeconomic conditions. [Published article abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0047272724000719), [revised NBER paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26405). **Abstract inspected; full specification-by-specification verification not completed.**

**Project synthesis:** These studies produce materially different reported results. They use different windows, counterfactuals, comparison groups and robustness criteria. Preserve all three with attributed claims and uncertainty; do **not** select a 'winning' study or average their estimates into a national employment elasticity.

## Infant-health results and explicit methodological challenge
- **Hoynes, Miller & Simon (2012), NBER W18206**: reports **6.7–10.8%** relative decline in low birth weight for a defined population, per $1,000 EITC increase. [NBER original abstract](https://www.nber.org/papers/w18206).
- **Hoynes, Miller & Simon (2015), *AEJ: Economic Policy* 7(1):172–211**: published headline **2–3%** relative decline in low birth weight per $1,000 treatment-on-treated. [Publisher article](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/pol.20120179). The changed estimate is a **version difference**, not a separate independent replication.
- **Dench & Joyce (2020), *Health Economics* 29(1):72–84**: question identification, pretrends/placebo tests, confounding, and mechanisms. [Published abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31758742/).
- **Hoynes/Miller/Simon response (2019)**: defend design and challenge critics' specifications. [Authors' reply](https://gspp.berkeley.edu/assets/uploads/research/pdf/DJ_response_NBER_112119_1.pdf). The response is a **researcher-authored rejoinder**, not an independently peer-reviewed adjudication.

**Project synthesis:** The published infant-health estimate is a reported causal finding under its authors' identification strategy, subject to a substantive methodological dispute. Retain both original versions and the critique/reply; avoid declaring either side conclusive.

## Incidence research: distinct types of evidence
- **Rothstein (2010)**: structural/model simulation of labor-market adjustment; preferred-parameter **$0.73 worker after-tax income per $1 EITC spending** is **not** an observed nationwide employer share. [Published article](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/pol.2.1.177).
- **Leigh (2010)**: observational econometric analysis of state EITC generosity and wages; study-specific wage associations are not a national capture fraction. [IZA paper](https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/4960/who-benefits-from-the-earned-income-tax-credit-incidence-among-recipients-coworkers-and-firms).

**Project synthesis:** legal claimant and ultimate economic beneficiary are different concepts. No audited, generalizable national employer/worker incidence split is available from this source set. **Unknown/insufficient evidence for national allocation** is compatible with presenting all named estimates.
