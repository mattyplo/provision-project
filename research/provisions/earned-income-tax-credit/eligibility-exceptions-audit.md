# EITC eligibility exceptions — primary-source inspection pass

**Reviewed:** 2026-11-16. **Important source vintage:** IRS [Publication 596 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p596) expressly says it is for **2025 returns**, not TY2026. This is a **mechanics/exception reconnaissance**, not a certification that every rule remains identical for TY2026. Verify against TY2026 instructions and 26 USC §32 before claiming final 2026 completeness.

## Exceptions and distinctions established in IRS Publication 596 (2025)
| Issue | IRS 2025 guidance | Publication implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qualifying-child SSN | Rule 2: a child without a valid SSN may still be listed on Schedule EIC; taxpayer may qualify for **self-only EIC**, if otherwise eligible. | Do **not** claim every qualifying child needs a valid SSN for taxpayer to receive *any* EIC. SSN is necessary to **count that child toward a higher child-based credit**. |
| Shared qualifying child | Rule 9: EIC may be claimed for the child by only one person; parent/nonparent and other tiebreaker rules apply. | A biological relationship alone does not establish entitlement when multiple eligible claimants exist. |
| Qualifying child | Rule 8: relationship, age, residence and joint-return tests; special treatment for adopted/foster children, military temporary absences, disability, births/deaths, and limited exceptions. | Avoid presenting the age/residence tests as exceptionless. |
| Separated spouse | Rule 3: married persons not filing jointly can qualify under special separated-spouse rules. | “Married filing separately never eligible” is false. |
| Military combat pay | Rule 7: qualifying nontaxable combat pay can be elected into earned income for EIC calculations; not every nontaxable military payment counts. | State that the **election** can alter EIC, not that combat pay automatically counts. |
| Clergy / self-employment | Rule 7 and Rule 15 discuss ministers' housing, clergy/church-employee earned-income calculations and Forms 4361/4029 exemptions. | EIC earned income is not always equivalent to W-2 wages or cash receipts. |
| Disability benefits | Rule 7 distinguishes certain pre-retirement-age employer disability payments from pensions and other non-earned payments. | Disability status alone does not decide earned-income treatment. |
| Previously disallowed EIC | “Disallowance of the EIC” section: Form 8862 can be required after disallowance; separate 2-year/10-year bans for reckless/intentional disregard or fraud. | Prior disallowance is not always a permanent exclusion; determine reason and applicable period. |
| Childless claimant | Rules 11–14: age, dependent/qualifying-child status and U.S. residence requirements. | Do not assume absence of a child eliminates EIC eligibility. |
| Foreign income and residence | Rules 4–5 distinguish full-year citizen/resident requirement, nonresident spouse election, and Form 2555 exclusion. | Foreign-income and residence questions require rule-specific analysis. |

## Additional nuance confirmed directly in IRS text
- Publication 596's introductory **Reminders** says a qualifying child who is awarded to another claimant by the tiebreaker rules can still leave a taxpayer eligible for the **self-only EIC**, if other conditions are satisfied.
- Rule 2 explicitly says children lacking valid SSNs may be listed on Schedule EIC while a self-only credit remains possible. This corrects overly broad language in prior project notes.
- Pub. 596 says EIC refunds generally do not count as income for specified federally financed assistance programs, with at least a 12-month resource disregard; this is an **administrative interaction**, not proof of who ultimately benefits economically.

## Publication gate

A publication-ready record can give a high-level summary with clear exceptions and a citation to §32, while linking to this audit and explicitly marking unverified 2026 details.
