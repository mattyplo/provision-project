# TY2026 EITC eligibility: statutory crosswalk and remaining administrative gaps

**Checked 2026-11-19.** Controlling statutory source: [OLRC 26 USC §32, laws through P.L. 119-124 (Oct. 15, 2026)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-124/t26/s32/). The project's [Oct. 16–31 enacted-law review](october-2026-cutoff-closeout.md) found no subsequent direct §32 amendment. Administrative cross-check: [IRS EITC eligibility page, accessed Nov. 19](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/individuals/earned-income-tax-credit/who-qualifies-for-the-earned-income-tax-credit-eitc), **mutable page**. ## Editorial corrections and caveats
- **Do not assert a qualifying child's missing SSN automatically makes the entire taxpayer ineligible.** §32(c)(3)(D) says the child is not counted under **§32(b)**; a taxpayer may qualify for the self-only rate if otherwise eligible. This is not an assertion that every taxpayer with such a child necessarily qualifies for self-only EITC.
- The IRS's general statement that a “child claimed” needs a valid SSN should be read together with §32(c)(3)(D)'s more precise legal consequence, rather than interpreted as overriding the statute.
- **Do not treat the 2021 §32(n) age-19 expansion as still applicable in 2026.**
- The IRS live page says taxpayers **may** need Schedule 3-A in TY2026. The [PRWORA audit](prwora-regulatory-status.md) distinguishes this from a final regulation by the Oct. 31 cutoff.

## Disposition
**C003: substantial statutory crosswalk completed; still PARTIAL for a fully certified TY2026 exception matrix.** Core eligibility and most edge cases are now directly supported by §32, reducing dependence on 2025 Publication 596. Remaining work is targeted: §152(c) qualifying-child crosswalk, special clergy/disability provisions, and final TY2026 administrative instructions/forms. These do not require withholding a clearly qualified high-level eligibility summary, but preclude labeling every exception verified.
