# Employment evidence: published robustness tables and conflicting specifications

**Audited:** 2026-11-16 (selected-table audit); publication QA follow-up 2026-11-19. **Scope:** published/author-hosted full texts and selected tables; no microdata replication. Study estimates are **researcher-reported**, not project estimates. Distinguish a published 2021 response to **Kleven (2019)** from Kleven's **2024 revised paper**.

## Eissa & Liebman (1996), QJE 111(2):605–637
[Full published paper hosted by Georgetown](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/noe/qje_596.pdf), pp. 627–630, Tables III–IV; [publisher](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/111/2/605/1938452).
- **Table III**, probit predicted effects: demographic controls **+1.9 pp** (SE **0.8 pp**); adding unemployment and AFDC controls **+2.6 pp**; adding state indicators **+2.8 pp**. These are effects of the **combined TRA1986 package**, not separately identified EITC effects.
- **Table III** first-child specification **+2.2 pp**; year interactions report **+0.8 pp in 1988** and **+2.9 pp by 1989–90**. Authors argue timing is consistent with learning about refunds, but other contemporaneous policies and differential trends cannot be fully ruled out.
- **Table IV** subgroup predicted participation effects: less than high school **+6.1 pp** (SE 2.4 pp), high school **+2.6 pp** (SE 1.4 pp), more than high school **+0.4 pp** (SE 1.1 pp); predicted in-range **+3.6 pp** (SE 1.2 pp), higher-income **−0.7 pp** (SE 1.6 pp). These are **heterogeneous estimates**; they do not demonstrate a uniform population-wide effect.
- **Version warning:** 1995 NBER W5158 abstract **+1.4 pp** (post 1988–89), published 1996 **up to +2.8 pp** (post 1988–90). Preserve both vintages.

## Schanzenbach & Strain (2021), Tax Policy and the Economy 35
[Published full text](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/epdf/10.1086/713494), **Table 3, pp. 107–109**, **Table 5, p. 114**.
- **Table 3, Panel A**: their preferred annual-employment specification finds statistically significant effects for 1975, 1986, 1990, 1993 but **not 2009**. Changing sample window, removing business-cycle and demographic controls, and switching annual to weekly employment progressively reduces significant episodes; **weekly outcome + limited controls leaves only 1993 significant**. **Panel B** adds higher-education women, likewise narrowing significant episodes. Authors explicitly report replicating Kleven (2019) coefficients with Kleven's choices. This is a **direct methodological reconciliation**, not merely an unexplained difference of opinion.
- **Table 5, Panel A**: pooled $1,000 increase in **maximum EITC** (2019 inflation-adjusted dollars) is associated with **+3.0, +3.0, +3.3 pp** annual employment in federal-only models, and **+2.6, +2.8, +2.9 pp** with federal/state EITC. Table includes models excluding welfare-waiver states or years 1994–99; **all six estimates significant at p<.01**, with state-clustered SE around 0.3 pp. This is **not** an effect of receiving $1,000 in actual EITC payments.
- **Panel B** including all education levels gives smaller pooled effects (**+1.7 to +2.2 pp**). Authors prefer annual employment and low-education samples and controls allowing the business cycle to affect mothers differently.
- **Limit:** pooling reform episodes can impose common functional relationships; differing treatment/control choices and policy timing matter. Report these as conditional study estimates, not incontrovertible EITC causal magnitudes.

## Kleven (May 2024 revision), The EITC and the Extensive Margin: A Reappraisal
[Author-hosted full May 2024 PDF](https://www.henrikkleven.com/uploads/3/7/3/1/37310663/kleven_eitc_may2024.pdf), **§5.4, Figure 8, pp. 24–25**, **§5.5, Figure 10**. [Published journal record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0047272724000719). **The inspected full text is the May 2024 author version**, not a certified line-for-line match to final journal typesetting.
- **432 specifications** from 7 reform experiments × 4 samples × 4 extensive-margin outcomes × 4 control sets; author notes online appendix provides individual event studies. This is more precise than saying only “about 500.”
- **Figure 8**: excluding 1993, mean reduced-form **−0.32 pp**, mean elasticity **−0.04**; for 1993 mean **+4.02 pp**, mean elasticity **+0.63**. These are **means across selected specifications**, not treatment estimates from a single preregistered model; averaging specifications does not produce a national causal elasticity.
- **Figure 10**: author characterizes prior positive elasticity estimates as outliers relative to his specification distribution, and examines confounding by welfare reforms and macroeconomic conditions. He explicitly acknowledges the grid is selected, not all logically possible specifications.
- **Limit:** a broad specification grid makes researcher degrees of freedom visible, but model choices are not necessarily equally credible. “Most estimates near zero” does **not** prove the true causal effect is zero.

## Precise methodological disagreement
| Design choice | Schanzenbach–Strain argument | Kleven reappraisal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employment outcome | Any work during year aligns with annual EITC | Compare annual and weekly outcomes |
| Population | Low-education unmarried women more likely exposed | Vary from all unmarried women to low-predicted-earnings subsets |
| Business cycle | Include state unemployment and child-status interaction | Vary control sets; concern over differential shocks |
| 1993 confounding | Exclude welfare-waiver states; estimates persist | Welfare reform/macroeconomy may explain exceptional 1993 pattern |
| Model uncertainty | Preferred specification and targeted checks | Wide specification distribution; large positive estimates outliers |

**Synthesis:** The studies **disagree in part because their chosen estimands, samples, outcome measures and identifying controls differ**. The disagreement is empirically testable and should be shown as a research feature. Neither 2021 nor 2024 is an independent randomized experiment, and neither proves the other fraudulent or worthless.
