# EITC qualifying-child statutory cross-reference — §§32(c)(3), 152(c)

**Legal cutoff:** 2026-10-31. **Verified:** 2026-11-19 against the Office of Law Revision Counsel editions of [26 USC §32](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-124/t26/s32/) and [26 USC §152](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-124/t26/s152/) (both updated through P.L. 119-124). See [October enacted-law cutoff review](october-2026-cutoff-closeout.md) for subsequent direct-§32 amendments. **Scope:** statutory qualifying-child rules, not every IRS processing instruction or fact-specific judicial interpretation.

## Incorporation rule — crucial exception
**§32(c)(3)(A)** adopts **§152(c)**'s qualifying-child definition **except** that **§152(c)(1)(D) (child's own support test) is disregarded** and **§152(e) (noncustodial-parent dependent release) is disregarded**. Consequently:
- **Do not apply §152(c)(1)(D)'s “child did not provide over half of own support” test to EITC.** This is a material exception to standard dependent eligibility.
- **A custodial parent's release of a dependency claim under §152(e) does not transfer the EITC qualifying-child claim to a noncustodial parent.** The EITC residence requirement still applies.
- **Do retain §152(c)(1)(E)'s joint-return restriction** (except a joint return filed only to claim a refund); §32's incorporation excludes **(D)**, not **(E)**.
- **§32(c)(3)(B)** adds a separate restriction on **married qualifying children**: generally not qualifying unless the taxpayer is entitled to a §151 deduction for the child (or would be but for §152(e)). This must be evaluated alongside §152(c)(1)(E), not collapsed into the ordinary joint-return rule.

## Nuances requiring precise public wording
1. **Age exception:** The disability rule overrides the ordinary **age paragraph**, including the “younger than taxpayer” criterion. Verify disability facts under §22(e)(3); do not call every disability qualifying.
2. **Tie-breaker vs waiver:** A noncustodial parent's Form 8332 or comparable release is for dependent-related benefits, **not** the EITC child claim. The §152(c)(4) priority applies when multiple taxpayers could qualify under the substantive rules; don't imply that a parent can always unilaterally choose a claimant.
3. **Shared home:** A child can meet residence for multiple household members. The tie-breaker resolves conflicting claims; parent/nonparent AGI conditions are not interchangeable.
4. **Special residence:** §152(f)(6) provides a **narrow kidnapped-child rule** expressly applicable to §32; §32(c)(4) provides a separate military exception. General temporary absences and births/deaths are handled in IRS guidance; **not independently verified in this statutory-only pass**.
5. **Married child:** Both §152(c)(1)(E) and §32(c)(3)(B) must be applied. A refund-only joint return exception under §152 does **not**, by itself, eliminate §32's married-child deduction condition.
6. **SSN:** Under §32(c)(3)(D), lack of child SSN prevents counting child for child-based amount; **self-only eligibility is separately tested**, and may be unavailable for other reasons. Do not promise self-only eligibility to every claimant.
